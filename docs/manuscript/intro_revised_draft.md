## Revised Draft

Adaptive immune responses unfold within organized tissue environments. B cells and T cells recognize antigen through clonally heritable adaptive immune receptors, making receptor sequence identity a molecular record of clonal expansion, selection, migration, and differentiation. In germinal centers (GCs), this record is inseparable from space: B cells diversify by somatic hypermutation, compete for affinity-dependent selection, cycle between light-zone and dark-zone states, and differentiate into memory B cells or antibody-secreting plasma cells \citep{Victora2014ClonalCellularDynamics,Victora2022GerminalCenters}. Yet GCs are not simply anatomical structures or uniform transcriptional compartments. Individual GCs can contain multiple competing clones, evolving subclades, and spatially structured lineage relationships. Understanding these responses therefore requires measurements that preserve both the receptor sequences that define lymphocyte clones and the tissue contexts in which those clones act.

Spatial transcriptomics has transformed tissue biology by enabling transcriptome-wide measurements to be interpreted in situ \citep{Stahl2016SpatialTranscriptomics,Tian2023ExpandingVistasSpatialTranscriptomics}. These assays relax the historical trade-off between broad molecular profiling and spatial localization, revealing cellular neighborhoods, tissue architecture, and local gene-expression programs. However, conventional spatial transcriptomic readouts do not, by themselves, recover the full receptor sequences that define adaptive immune clones. Conversely, adaptive immune receptor repertoire sequencing (AIRR-seq) and established AIRR analysis frameworks can resolve V(D)J usage, clonal expansion, somatic mutation, class switching, clone membership, and BCR lineage history \citep{Rubelt2017AIRRRecommendations,VanderHeiden2018AIRRStandardizedRepresentations,VanderHeiden2014Presto,Gupta2015ChangeO,Nouri2018Scoper,Ye2013IgBLAST}. BCR phylogenetic methods further show how receptor lineages can be used to infer migration, differentiation, class switching, and time-resolved evolution \citep{Hoehn2022BCellPhylogenetics,Fielding2025TyCHE}. However, most repertoire workflows begin from dissociated or bulk samples and therefore lose the physical locations where these immune processes occur. This separation is particularly limiting in lymphoid and inflamed tissues, where clonal expansion, cell-cell interaction, local antigen exposure, and lineage diversification are spatially coupled.

Recent spatial AIRR-seq, or spAIRR, technologies directly address this gap by recovering immune receptor sequences together with tissue coordinates. Spatial VDJ maps immunoglobulin and T cell receptor sequences alongside morphology and whole-transcriptome measurements, enabling analyses of receptor pairing, tumor-associated clonal expansion, and B cell lineage trajectories \citep{Engblom2023SpatialVDJ}. Slide-TCR-seq and related spatial TCR approaches localize TCR clonotypes with gene expression, revealing spatially segregated T cell clones in lymphoid tissues and cancer \citep{Liu2022SlideTCRseq,Hudson2022VisiumTCR,Benotmane2023SPTCRseq}. Stereo-XCR-seq extends spatial immune repertoire profiling toward single-cell-resolution TCR and BCR sequencing in immune disorders, identifying lymphoid aggregates with distinct clonal activities \citep{Yu2025StereoXCRseq}. AIR-SPACE combines high-resolution spatial transcriptomics with paired long-read BCR and TCR sequencing to resolve time-dependent adaptive immune responses, GC substructure, and clonal sharing across GCs \citep{Jiang2026AIRSPACE}. Together, these methods show that receptor identity can turn spatial omics from descriptive tissue mapping into clonotype-, lineage-, and repertoire-resolved analysis of adaptive immunity.

As these experimental methods mature, a second bottleneck has become apparent: spAIRR data are difficult to process and interpret reproducibly. As in AIRR-seq more broadly, the ability to generate large, complex datasets can outpace the infrastructure needed to standardize, annotate, share, and analyze them \citep{Rubelt2017AIRRRecommendations,VanderHeiden2018AIRRStandardizedRepresentations}. For spAIRR data, receptor-containing long reads must be demultiplexed, corrected, assigned to spatial barcodes, annotated with V(D)J and constant-region information, filtered into reliable AIRR tables, and linked back to spatial transcriptomic objects. Once processed, those data must be visualized on tissue, summarized across anatomical or user-defined regions, and analyzed for clonal diversity, spatial localization, receptor-chain pairing, and lineage structure. RAD addresses an important upstream component of this process by providing a read-structure-agnostic demultiplexer for single-cell and spatial long-read sequencing data, with flexible barcode extraction and correction across complex read layouts \citep{RAD2026}. LongAIRR similarly reflects the need for computational strategies tailored to spatial AIRR data from long-read sequencing \citep{LongAIRR2026}. Other tools solve focused downstream problems, including SPaCeD, which infers heavy-light chain pairing from spatial BCR-seq by integrating expression and spatial point-pattern information \citep{SPaCeD2026}. However, spAIRR studies still lack a unified, reusable analysis environment that connects raw spatial receptor sequencing output to coordinate-aware AIRR tables, spatial transcriptomic objects, region-level tissue analysis, clone-localization statistics, receptor-pairing workflows, and lineage-aware visualization.

Here, we introduce SIRAP, an Immcantation package for processing, integrating, visualizing, and analyzing spAIRR data. SIRAP is designed to extend, rather than replace, established AIRR infrastructure. It interfaces with RAD for read-structure-agnostic demultiplexing of long-read spatial sequencing data, supports downstream pRESTO/IgBLAST-style processing into AIRR-formatted receptor tables, and works with Immcantation tools such as scoper and dowser for clone calling and BCR lineage reconstruction \citep{RAD2026,VanderHeiden2014Presto,Ye2013IgBLAST,Nouri2018Scoper,Hoehn2022BCellPhylogenetics}. SIRAP then adds the spatial operations needed to interpret receptor repertoires in tissue: mapping receptor sequences to coordinates, integrating AIRR tables with spatial transcriptomic objects, visualizing BCR and TCR clones in histological context, summarizing clonal structure across regions, quantifying clone localization, pairing lineage trees with spatial clone maps, supporting SPaCeD-style receptor-chain pairing, and tracking clones across tissue and peripheral compartments. By combining common AIRR representations with tissue-aware analysis, SIRAP provides an end-to-end bridge between spAIRR data generation and biological interpretation.

We demonstrate SIRAP by analyzing receptor-level organization in ectopic thymic GCs from myasthenia gravis (MG) and canonical GCs. MG provides a compelling disease context because the thymus can contain lymphofollicular hyperplasia, ectopic GCs, and B cells or plasma cells capable of producing disease-associated autoantibodies \citep{Vinuesa2009AutoimmuneGCs,Jiang2020MGThymusClones}. The MG thymus can act as a reservoir of antigen-experienced B cells, and repertoire studies have shown that thymus-associated B cell clones can persist in circulation before and after thymectomy, suggesting exchange between ectopic lymphoid tissue and peripheral blood \citep{Jiang2020MGThymusClones}. Using SIRAP, we compare ectopic and canonical GC contexts by quantifying BCR clonal dominance, diversity, spatial localization, and lineage structure. This analysis tests whether ectopic thymic GCs in MG resemble canonical GCs in receptor-level organization and whether spatial clonal structure provides evidence for local antigen-driven selection and tissue-periphery clonal connections. By unifying spatial receptor processing, Immcantation-based AIRR analysis, and tissue-aware visualization and statistics, SIRAP provides a reproducible framework for turning spAIRR data into biological insight.
